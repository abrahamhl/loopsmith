# Field notes from one job

**Scope, stated up front:** everything below comes from a **single job** — one scenario, one
session, **two clips** generated by Kling 3.0 (image-to-video, 1080p, 16:9, two chained 15-second
runs). Two clips is not a sample. Nothing here should be read as a property of the model, of
generative video in general, or of anything beyond these two files.

It is published because concrete numbers from a real job are more useful than none, and because
the *measurement recipes* at the end transfer even when the numbers don't. If your figures differ,
that is the expected outcome, not a contradiction.

---

## What was observed

### Duration did not match the request

A run requested at 15 seconds came back as **361 frames at 24 fps = 15.041667 s** — one frame more
than 15 × 24. Both clips, same result.

Two runs give 722 frames. A 30-second loop at 24 fps needs 720. Plain concatenation lands at
30.083 s.

This is the observation with the most practical bite: it is not a subtle quality issue, it is an
arithmetic one, and it does not go away by being careful.

### Frame size was not 16:9

Both files measured **1916×1080**. That is an aspect of 1.7741 against 16:9's 1.7778. Players
stretch it silently.

Worth noting for anyone correcting this: the ideal crop height at this size is 1077.75. Rounding
*down* to 1076 discards two more rows than necessary and takes the aspect error from 0.02 % to
0.16 %. Round to the nearest even value.

### The final frame moved less than its neighbours — by an inconsistent amount

Comparing the last frame step against the two before it:

| | Second-to-last steps | Final step | Final as % of neighbours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Run 1 | 1.222 | 1.152 | **94 %** |
| Run 2 | 0.378 | 0.310 | **82 %** |

Both slower, but 6 % and 18 % are not the same finding, and two data points spanning that range
support no generalisation at all.

There is also an uncontrolled variable: run 2's prompt explicitly asked the camera to settle into
a static hero composition, and run 1's did not. The deceleration may be the model following
instructions rather than an artefact of end-frame conditioning. **This was not isolated.** Testing
it would need runs with identical prompts differing only in whether the ending is asked to settle.

### Dropping the first frame of the second run improved the join

Measured against a local normal step of about 1.36:

| Join | Difference |
|---|---|
| Run 1's last frame → run 2's **first** frame | 1.976 |
| Run 1's last frame → run 2's **second** frame | 1.882 |

Dropping it measurably helped. **An earlier draft of this document explained that as the first
frame being the input still re-encoded, with different grain. That explanation is wrong** — it was
tested and failed. High-frequency energy across the opening frames:

| | f000 | f001 | f002 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Run 1 | 2.440 | 2.443 | 2.437 |
| Run 2 | 2.565 | 2.597 | 2.621 |

Flat in run 1, smoothly rising in run 2. No discontinuity at the first frame in either. Whatever
causes the join to improve, it is not a texture break.

A more plausible mechanism, still untested: when two runs are chained through a shared anchor
image, run 1's *generated* approach to that anchor and run 2's *literal copy* of it are two
different pictures of the same intended moment. The mismatch is in how closely the model lands on
its end frame — which would vary per generation, and is a reason to measure rather than assume.

### The arithmetic and the cut coincided

361 + 359 = **720 frames = 30.000000 s at 24 fps.**

The two frames that had to go for the duration to be exact were, on this job, also the two whose
removal improved the seams. That is a pleasing coincidence and it is what made the result clean.
It is not a law — it happened to work out at this length, at this frame rate, with this surplus.

### What did not break

Stated because it saved time not to chase it:

- **Colour held.** Mean luma stepped 0.46/255 at the join and 0.21/255 at the loop, against a
  perceptual threshold around 1.0. No flash, no grade drift between runs.
- **Camera speed held across the join.** Motion energy went 1.20 → 1.15 across the cut — the seam
  moves like any other frame. That join is genuinely invisible.

### What could not be fixed by cutting

The loop point joined a shot decelerating to near-rest to a shot already in motion. Position was
continuous and colour matched, but speed stepped by roughly a factor of two.

No choice of cut fixes this; the missing motion was never generated. The fix is upstream, in
prompting the runs to end and begin at comparable speeds.

## Measurement recipes

These transfer regardless of model or footage, and are the part of this document worth keeping.

**Motion energy per frame** — absolute difference between consecutive frames, downscaled, mean
luma. One pass gives you freezes, cuts and deceleration across a whole clip. Downscaling suppresses
compression noise while preserving real motion.

```
ffprobe -v error -f lavfi \
  -i "movie=clip.mp4,scale=320:180,tblend=all_mode=difference,signalstats" \
  -show_entries frame_tags=lavfi.signalstats.YAVG -of csv=p=0
```

**Difference between two arbitrary frames** — the same metric, so values are comparable to the
curve above:

```
ffprobe -v error -f lavfi \
  -i "movie=a.png[a];movie=b.png[b];[a]scale=320:180[x];[b]scale=320:180[y];[x][y]blend=all_mode=difference,signalstats" \
  -show_entries frame_tags=lavfi.signalstats.YAVG -of csv=p=0
```

**Colour drift** — `signalstats` YAVG / UAVG / VAVG on boundary frames.

**Texture** — a frame minus its own Gaussian blur. Catches grain changes and re-encoded stills:

```
ffprobe -v error -f lavfi \
  -i "movie=f.png,split[a][b];[a]gblur=sigma=1.5[c];[b][c]blend=all_mode=difference,signalstats" \
  -show_entries frame_tags=lavfi.signalstats.YAVG -of csv=p=0
```

## Rules of thumb

Working thresholds, arrived at on this job. Starting points, not standards.

| Measure | Looked fine | Worth investigating |
|---|---|---|
| Seam difference ÷ local normal step | 0.75 – 1.35 | below 0.50 (freeze) or above 1.60 (jump) |
| Mean luma step at a seam | under 1.0 / 255 | over 2.0 / 255 |
| Frozen frames | none | any run of 6 or more |
| Re-encode fidelity against source | above 45 dB PSNR | below 40 dB |

## One methodological warning

PSNR is unreliable for comparing frames of *different* content. It is content-dependent: a dark,
low-detail passage scores high for reasons unrelated to continuity. On this job it suggested a
frozen tail that the motion-energy metric showed was merely 18 % slow, and that wrong reading
survived two rounds of analysis before being caught.

Use PSNR for encode fidelity, where source and target are the same image. Use absolute difference
for continuity.

## Addendum: cutting on motion

A second job (two 10-second runs, 720p, same model) produced a case the seam-only
search could not solve well.

Available: 482 frames. Target: 480. Two frames had to go, and the only cut positions
were the head and tail of clip 2.

| Plan | Junction | Loop |
|---|---|---|
| drop 2 from tail | 1.02 (excellent) | **2.34** |
| drop 1 + 1 | 2.06 | 1.68 |
| drop 2 from head | 2.93 | 1.09 |

Every option damaged something. The optimiser picked the lowest total cost — correctly,
by its own metric — and that meant a **2.34 ratio at the loop point**: a visible jump,
in a slow passage, repeating on every cycle.

The fix was to stop cutting at the seams. Dropping the two frames **inside clip 1, at its
fastest-moving passage**, left both seams untouched:

| | Junction | Loop |
|---|---|---|
| Seam cuts | 1.02 | 2.34 |
| **Cut on motion** | **1.02** | **1.09** |

Measured on the assembled result, the interior cuts register as a 12.7 spike against
neighbours of 7.3 — a ratio of 1.74. Worse, numerically, than the loop error it replaced.
It is not worse to watch.

### Why the numbers mislead here

The cost function normalises by the local frame step, which corrects for magnitude but not
for **visibility**. Two effects it does not model:

**Speed hides error.** The eye cannot track detail through fast motion, and the footage
itself carries motion blur. A 1.74 ratio inside a passage moving at twice the clip's mean
reads as nothing; a 2.34 ratio in a passage moving at half the mean reads as a jump.

**The loop seam is not one seam among many.** An interior cut is seen once per cycle and
sits in the middle of continuous action. The loop seam is the join the viewer returns to
on every repetition, and it usually falls in a settling passage, because that is where
generated clips are anchored.

### Proposed weighting, not yet validated

```
visibility ≈ |ratio − 1| × w(local_speed)     with w decreasing as speed rises
```

A first approximation is `w = 1 / (1 + local_energy / mean_energy)`, which gives a passage
at twice the mean a third of the weight of one at half the mean. This is reasoning from the
one case above, **not a measured psychophysical result**, and it should be calibrated
against side-by-side viewing before anyone trusts it.

`--motion-cut` implements the strategy; the weighting above is not yet in the cost function,
so the choice between strategies is currently the operator's, not the tool's.
